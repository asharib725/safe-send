Safe Send

A send-money flow that checks every transfer for scam patterns before you confirm. Rules decide the risk. AI only explains it, in English or Urdu.

01 · PROBLEM

Most wallet fraud isn't hacking. It's persuasion.

A call about a prize, a BISP payment or a "wrong transfer", and the account owner sends the money themselves. Pakistan's central bank names fake prize notifications and BISP payment claims among the common tactics, and calls customers the "first line of defence".2 Policy researchers make the same point: many scams target people, not technology.3

Instant payments make this worse. Once money moves, it usually can't be pulled back, so the send screen is the last moment a product can help.

$9.3B

Estimated yearly scam losses in Pakistan, about 2.5% of GDP.1

46M

Raast IDs in use, so most people can be paid by mobile number.4

~17 s

For a Raast payment to reach the other person, per an SBP presentation.5

02 · THE CORE DECISION

Rules decide. AI explains.

A model deciding whether you may send money is hard to test and harder to explain. So risk comes from data the wallet already has. AI does the part it's good at: turning those signals into one or two plain sentences a person will actually read mid-payment.

Detection · deterministic

Rules find the signals

Transfer history, reports from other users, account age and the wording of the note. Testable, explainable and the same every time.

→
Explanation · AI, constrained

AI writes the sentence

From the detected signals only, in English or Urdu. It can't add a signal, change the level or block a payment.

03 · FRICTION THAT MATCHES RISK

Paying family stays one slide. A scam pattern gets a firm stop.

Clear

No extra step. The check runs in about a second and gets out of the way.

Check

A new number gets a "call them first" option and its reasons on request, not a wall.

Stop

"Don't send" becomes the main action. Sending anyway takes a confirmation, never a block.

04 · GUARDRAILS

What the AI is never allowed to say

Five checks run on every explanation before anyone sees it. If one fails, the user gets the approved fallback instead.

  • ✕No safety claims. Never call a transfer or a person safe, verified or guaranteed.
  • ✕No accusations. Describe the pattern, never call a person a scammer.
  • ✕No amounts. The user decides how much to send.
  • ✕No certainty. Patterns can be wrong.
  • ✕Two sentences at most. People read this mid-payment.
You're safe to send this.Blocked: promises safety the system can't know.
K. Ahmed is a scammer, don't pay him.Blocked: accuses a named person.
Try sending Rs 2,000 instead.Blocked: suggests an amount.
Our AI is 100% sure this is fraud.Blocked: claims certainty.

The rules aren't a slide. The prototype's AI rules tab runs every approved explanation and every rejected draft through the same validator, live in the browser.

05 · LANGUAGE

Urdu, written for reading mid-payment

The explanation and its reasons switch to Urdu in a Nastaliq face, right-aligned, in plain words. The people most likely to get a prize call are the least likely to read a warning in English.

Not tested with users yet. That's the first study I'd run.
06 · SMALL DECISIONS

The details that carry the design

Never "safe"

The clear state says "No warning signs found", because the system can't know a transfer is safe.

Phrases, not words

"Claim fee" raises a flag. "School fee" sent to family doesn't.

A tap can't send

Sending takes a drag. A single tap on the slider only nudges it.

No drag required

Keyboard and screen-reader users confirm with Enter.

Errors talk about money

"Nothing left your account and your balance hasn't changed."

Reasons on request

"Why am I seeing this?" lists the exact signals behind every warning.

07 · BUILT AS A SYSTEM

One source of truth, in code and in Figma

One tokens file feeds both the CSS variables and the Figma variables, under the same names. The 13 components exist once in code and once in Figma, and each Figma component's description carries its code signature. Changes go in through pull requests, and every merge redeploys the live prototype.

08 · HOW I'D KNOW IT WORKS

What I'd measure

  • Share of transfers to reported numbers that end in "Don't send".
  • False alarms on people you've paid before. Target: close to zero.
  • Time added to a clear transfer. Target: under 1.5 seconds.
  • Whether Urdu explanations change what people do.
This is a concept. None of it is measured yet.
09 · WHAT I LEFT OUT

What I chose not to do

  • No risk score. "73% risk" invites false precision. Three plain levels don't.
  • No chatbot. Sending money isn't a conversation.
  • No hard blocks. People pay new contacts every day. The user stays in control.
  • No real brand. Independent concept, fictional names and numbers.
